Issue 09
15 – 21 Nov 2026
18 picks · 6 columns

This week agents were tested less as tools and more as colleagues. A twelve-week study followed 936 AI scientist agents from 35 labs as they published, reviewed and corrected each other, and SWE-CoWork found that the best coding agents pass most unit tests yet complete fewer than 8% of assignments inside a simulated company. OpenAI released GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol at lower prices, and MBZUAI opened K2-Horizon, six models from 0.9B to 375B with every checkpoint and dataset. In vision, Apple's FastFrames brings autoregressive video to 36 ms a frame, while the Flip Test finds that video world models still misread physical state. On devices, a 27B model holds up on agent tasks below 3 bits, and Google's G-MTP drafters double Gemma 4 decoding speed on phones. In infrastructure, TideCache streams attention cache from host memory for hybrid models, and vLLM fuses whole decode layers into one or two kernels on AMD GPUs.

      • Timeout Cascades for coding agents ↗ (Zhou, Sviridov, Ustiugov and Javaid, 18 Nov): tries a small open-weight model first and escalates to a large one only when it times out. The authors report up to 33% lower GPU-hour cost on SWE-bench Verified with no loss of accuracy.
      • CECAR ↗ (KAIST, Yonsei University and the Agency for Defense Development, 15 Nov): cache-aware expert routing that biases a mixture-of-experts model towards experts already in GPU memory. The authors report Qwen3-30B-A3B at 12.5 tokens per second on a 16 GB GPU with experts stored on SSD, 4.5 times conventional MoE inference.
      • XM-Sparse ↗ (Netpreme, HKUST and NUS, 17 Nov): offloads both the KV cache and the sparse-attention index cache to a scale-up memory pool, built on SGLang. The authors report up to 4.98 times the decode throughput of PCIe-based ECHO on DeepSeek-V3.2 and GLM-5.3, using an emulated pool.
