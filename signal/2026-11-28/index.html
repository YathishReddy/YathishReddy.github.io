Issue 10
22 – 28 Nov 2026
18 picks · 6 columns

This week several papers looked beneath the headline scores. A Technion and Google Research study fine-tunes a model to follow a hidden five-step rule that a chain-of-thought monitor misses, ShellBench finds that the same model scores differently depending on the agent harness around it, and an INT8 study shows converters that keep accuracy while changing 2.4 to 4.1% of individual answers. Z.ai rebuilt its GLM-5.4 flagship as a looped transformer, and Yandex opened an 80B base model that uses 3B parameters per token. In vision, a memory agent keeps live video stories consistent, while a Princeton and Meta study finds that generated helper images mostly lower visual-reasoning accuracy. On devices, MixMoMo runs 35B to 80B expert models on Snapdragon phones by giving each expert its own precision; among small models, 31M encoders both classify and embed; and in infrastructure NVIDIA trains a 2T expert model at 13 exaFLOP/s with Generalized Tensor Parallelism.

    Also shipped this week

    • GPT-6 protein design ↗ (OpenAI, 24 Nov): OpenAI reports that GPT-6 models can design protein shapes and sequences through reasoning and code, without calling specialised biology models for the designs. In lab tests with a partner, 35 of 322 minibinder designs (10.9%) bound their targets; the company notes that specialised models were still used to filter candidates and that full structure prediction remains weak.
    • Shirudo ↗ (OpenAI and Cisco, 23 Nov): an open-source Linux runtime that lets agents write temporary eBPF "shields" to block an exploit on a running system while a permanent patch is prepared. The companies report that a Codex-written shield stopped a Java4Shell exploit against a live Elasticsearch service with zero restarts; the code is BSD 2-Clause licensed.
    • Sarvam-105B-1711 ↗ (Sarvam AI, 22 Nov): a new open-weight checkpoint of Sarvam's 105B expert model, aimed at high-volume work such as customer support and voice agents, with context extended to 160K tokens. The company reports that the NVFP4 version is 65 GB and fits on a single 96 GB workstation GPU, at about $0.15 per million tokens.
